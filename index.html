Clinical interpretation

Essential comparisons

Example Classical circuit explanation Clinical association
Parkinson disease Reduced SNc dopamine weakens D1-mediated facilitation and reduces D2-mediated suppression of indirect-pathway neurons. Bradykinesia and hypokinetic motor dysfunction. Tremor and rigidity are not generated by this model.
Early Huntington disease Preferential early dysfunction/loss of indirect-pathway striatal projection neurons reduces suppression of unwanted actions. Chorea. Cognitive and psychiatric manifestations and later-stage disease are outside this model.
Unilateral STN lesion Reduced excitatory drive to GPi/SNr decreases inhibitory output to the thalamus in the classical model. Contralateral hemiballismus/hemichorea. Similar movements can arise from lesions elsewhere in the network.

Model assumptions

This is a feed-forward educational implementation of the classical rate model. GPi/SNr and GPe have tonic baseline activity. Inhibiting an inhibitory output can disinhibit the next structure; an inhibitory synapse does not thereby become an excitatory synapse.

Direct and indirect pathways are not simply isolated “movement on” and “movement off” switches. They can be coactive during behavior and participate in action selection. The hyperdirect pathway provides a relatively rapid cortical route to basal ganglia output via STN; its functional roles are more complex than a universal emergency brake.

GPi and SNr are grouped as output nuclei, although their projections and functions are not identical. The diagram represents a motor thalamocortical loop. Additional pallidal, striatal, brainstem, and recurrent connections, spatially organized action channels, and nonmotor loops are omitted.

Moving dots indicate the direction and sign of connections. Their speed and number do not represent conduction delays, firing frequency, or synaptic strength. Node brightness and bars reflect arbitrary model activity.

Disease presets illustrate selected circuit mechanisms. They are not complete disease models. The simulator does not predict clinical severity, reproduce movement waveforms, simulate treatment, or equate DBS with a destructive lesion.

View model equations

All values are clipped to the range 0–1. C = cortical input; D = dopamine parameter; I = indirect population function; S = STN function. Coefficients are illustrative. 

D1 = C × (0.30 + 0.70D)
D2 = C × (1 − 0.60D) × I
GPe = clip(0.80 − 0.70D2)
STN = clip(0.15 + 0.65(1 − GPe) + 0.30C) × S
GPi/SNr = clip(0.50 + 0.60STN − 0.60D1)
Thalamus = clip(0.95 − 0.80GPi/SNr)

These equations encode selected signs and trends. Thalamic activity is not equivalent to movement amplitude. The displayed cortical return connection is not solved as a recurrent feedback system.
