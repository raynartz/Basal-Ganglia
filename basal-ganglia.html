Clinical explanation

Points to remember

GPi/SNr normally provide tonic inhibitory output. If that output decreases, the thalamus is disinhibited. Disinhibition is release from inhibition; it does not change GABA into an excitatory neurotransmitter.

In the classical model, SNc dopamine facilitates D1-associated direct-pathway activity and suppresses D2-associated indirect-pathway activity. These modulatory effects are explained in the text rather than drawn as additional crossing connections.

Direct and indirect pathways can be coactive during behavior. “Facilitation” and “suppression” here describe simplified circuit effects, not independent movement switches. Hyperdirect signaling provides a cortical route to STN that bypasses the striatum.

The clinical arrows illustrate selected classical mechanisms. They do not model firing patterns, synchrony, action channels, clinical severity, treatment effects, or the full motor and nonmotor manifestations of disease. GPi and SNr are grouped for this motor-loop illustration.
