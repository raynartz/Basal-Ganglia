Model scope and references

This is the classical qualitative circuit model, not a numerical simulation or a measurement of firing rates. Dot frequencies are illustrative. Dots indicate total population signaling, including loss of neurons, rather than firing of an individual surviving neuron. Pathway highlighting does not turn other pathways off.

Normal pathway views show the net changes associated with activation of the selected route, relative to its reference state. Clinical views show a simplified disease pattern relative to normal under comparable cortical input; changing the highlighted route does not change that pattern. Step buttons explain anatomical and synaptic relationships; they do not simulate physiological transmission delays.

Direct and indirect populations can be coactive. The real network includes additional projections, heterogeneous cells, timing, oscillations, and plasticity. This model does not explain all parkinsonian symptoms, late Huntington disease, dystonia, or the mechanisms of deep brain stimulation. GPi and SNr are grouped for the teaching model; their outputs are not identical.

SNc dopamine generally facilitates D1 direct-pathway signaling and suppresses D2 indirect-pathway signaling. This modulatory influence is described in the clinical panel to keep the diagram readable.
